NBA Shot Quality & Impact Model

I built a model that scores how hard every NBA shot is (xShot) and used it to find who makes shots they shouldn't. Separately, a lineup-level regression measures how much each player actually moves his team's scoring margin.

1 · Who makes their team better?

 

Impact is a player's RAPM (Regularized Adjusted Plus-Minus): his estimated effect on his team's scoring margin per 100 possessions, after accounting for every teammate and opponent on the floor. It's pooled over three seasons and anchored to box-score stats for stability. 0 is average. +2 is a very good starter.

How this works

Data

Play-by-play for every regular-season and playoff game from 2014-15 to 2025-26, pulled from the NBA Stats API (nba_api) into PostgreSQL: about 7.5M events and 2.7M field-goal attempts with location, shot type and game clock.

xShot: how hard is this shot?

An XGBoost classifier predicts the probability a shot goes in using only what's known before the shot: location (distance, angle, zone, corner three), shot type (dunk, layup, pull-up, step-back, floater… 17 flags) and context (period, clock, overtime, playoffs). It does not know who is shooting, so it measures difficulty, not shooter skill, the same idea as expected goals (xG) in soccer.

Trained on 2014-15 → 2022-23 and tested on 2023-24 → 2024-25 (time-based split, so it never sees the future). Test log loss is 0.638 vs. 0.691 for predicting league-average FG% on every shot (7.7% better), with a Brier score of 0.225.

Shot-making vs. expected

For each player, actual FG% minus the average xShot of his attempts. Positive means he converts more than an average shooter would on the same shots. Players need 300+ attempts in a regular season (75+ in the playoffs) to be shown.

Impact: RAPM (Regularized Adjusted Plus-Minus)

The game is split into 422K stints, stretches where the same ten players are on the floor. A ridge regression then estimates each player's effect on the scoring margin per 100 possessions while controlling for everyone else on the court. Ratings pool three seasons of stints and use a box-score prior (per-minute plus-minus), which cuts noise by about 40% compared with single seasons.

In progress: xRAPM. The same regression with every shot re-scored by its xShot value instead of make/miss, to separate process from shooting luck. The current version shrinks too hard toward zero to rank players credibly, so it's being retuned before it goes on this page.

Limitations

xShot has no tracking data (defender distance, shooter speed), so "difficulty" is location- and play-type-based. Ridge shrinkage pulls everyone toward 0, so the ratings here run smaller than on public RAPM sites. Treat small differences as ties.
